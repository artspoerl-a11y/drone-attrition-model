The model

One step is one month. Factories are destroyed and repaired as in Policy Tensor's model. New in this version, depot strikes remove a share of the stockpile each month before launches.

N(t+1) = N(t) − d_fac·N(t) + r_fac·(N_total − N(t)) f_actual(t) = min( f, I(t)·(1 − d_inv) + k·N(t) ) I(t+1) = I(t)·(1 − d_inv) + k·N(t) − f_actual(t) N* = r_fac / (d_fac + r_fac) · N_total f_sustain = k · N*

Months of full-rate fire counts consecutive months, from the start, in which the full desired rate f can be launched.

This measures how long a tempo could be sustained, not how long it will be. In the 2026 campaign launches fell sharply after the first weeks while thousands of drones reportedly remained, whether from degraded launch capacity or deliberate rationing. A rationed stockpile sits in depots longer, so depot strikes take a larger share of it.

Assumptions: dinv is constant over time and applies only to inventory carried into the month, not to new production in transit.

Where the inputs come from

No public figure exists for Iran's drone inventory before the campaign began on February 28, 2026. Published estimates range from several thousand to well above 10,000. The 20,000 baseline sits at the high end of that range and the 10,000 Extreme scenario sits mid-range.

  • Stockpile. "Several thousand to well above 10,000 drones" (Grieco, War on the Rocks, Mar 16, 2026). "A total force numbering in the thousands" (Nadimi, Washington Institute, May 22, 2026). Israeli officials say Iran likely has more drones than its roughly 2,500 ballistic missiles (SAN).
  • Production. Industrial capacity of around 10,000 per month, per the Centre for Information Resilience (reported Mar 22, 2026). As a proxy, Russia's Alabuga plant built 5,760 Shahed-type drones from January to September 2024 (ISW) and about 2,800 per month by 2026 (Militarnyi).
  • Launches. More than 2,000 drones by March 3, per CENTCOM (DefenseScoop); open-source tallies counted 1,522 by March 5 (Hudson). Roughly 4,400 by the April 7 ceasefire, 85–90% of them in the first two to three weeks (Washington Institute).

Launch counts set a floor on the stockpile, not a ceiling.
